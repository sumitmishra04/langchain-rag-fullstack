The Tortoise and the Rabbit

Once upon a time, in a lush green forest, there lived a rabbit named Rocket and a tortoise named Steady. Rocket was known throughout the forest for his incredible speed. He could zip past the oak trees, leap over the brook, and dash through the meadows faster than any other animal. He was proud of his speed — perhaps a little too proud.

Steady, on the other hand, moved slowly and deliberately. He never rushed. The other animals sometimes laughed at how long it took him to cross the forest path. But Steady never let it bother him. He simply kept moving, one step at a time.

One sunny morning, Rocket was boasting loudly near the old oak tree.

"I am the fastest animal in this entire forest!" he announced. "No one can beat me in a race. Not the fox, not the deer, not anyone!"

Steady was sitting nearby, munching on a leaf.

"I will race you," said Steady quietly.

The forest went silent. Then the animals burst into laughter.

Rocket stared at the tortoise, then burst out laughing himself.

"You? Race me? Oh, this I have to see," he said, wiping tears from his eyes.

The race was set for the next morning. The course went from the old oak tree, through the meadow, past the brook, and up the hill to the large boulder at the top. The wise owl would serve as the judge.

The morning of the race, every animal in the forest gathered to watch. The owl called them to the starting line.

"Ready?" said the owl. "Go!"

Rocket shot off like an arrow. Within seconds, he was already halfway through the meadow. Steady began walking steadily from the starting line, one slow step after another.

Rocket looked back and could barely see Steady in the distance. He laughed to himself.

"This is too easy," he thought. "I have so much time. I'll take a little nap under that shady tree by the brook. I'll still finish long before that slow tortoise."

Rocket curled up under a cool, shady tree near the brook and closed his eyes. The warm sun and gentle breeze quickly lulled him into a deep sleep.

Meanwhile, Steady kept walking. He crossed the meadow. He passed the brook, stepping carefully over the stones. He began climbing the hill, breathing steadily, never stopping, never looking back.

The animals watching gasped as Steady passed the sleeping Rocket without a sound.

Up and up the hill Steady climbed, slow and sure. And then — he reached the large boulder at the top.

The owl declared, "Steady the Tortoise wins the race!"

A huge cheer erupted from the forest. The noise woke Rocket with a start. He looked around, confused, then spotted Steady standing proudly at the finish line at the top of the hill.

Rocket's ears dropped. He had lost.

He ran up the hill as fast as he could, but it was too late. Steady had already won.

Rocket walked up to Steady and lowered his head.

"I underestimated you," said Rocket. "I was arrogant and lazy. You deserved to win."

Steady smiled kindly.

"You are faster than me, Rocket. You always will be. But speed means nothing if you do not stay in the race."

From that day on, Rocket never boasted again. And whenever he felt like giving up or slacking off, he remembered the tortoise who never stopped walking.

The end.

Moral: Slow and steady wins the race. Consistency and persistence beat raw talent that goes to waste.
